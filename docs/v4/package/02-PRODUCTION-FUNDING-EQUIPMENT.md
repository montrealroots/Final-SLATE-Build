# Tailored Production, Family Funds And Equipment

## Independent choices compose a pipeline

Format (short/feature/series/limited and supported nonfiction), method (live action/2D/3D/stop motion/hybrid), source basis and financing arrangement are independent axes. Share accounting, events and scheduling; specialize work graphs. Do not duplicate engines or make the same task list with renamed labels.

Live action: development, breakdown, financing, casting, locations, design, rehearsals, equipment tests, scheduling, capture, footage management, editing, effects where applicable, sound/music, finish and delivery.

2D animation: development, visual design, boards, animatic, voice strategy, layout, backgrounds, selected hand-drawn/rigged animation workflow, cleanup where required, compositing, sound/music and delivery.

3D animation: development, boards/animatic, models/materials, rigs, layout, animation, simulations where required, lighting, rendering, compositing, sound/music and delivery.

Stop motion: boards/animatic, puppet/set design, armatures, fabrication, facial method, tests, stage/camera setup, frame capture, continuity, maintenance/replacements, compositing, sound/music and delivery. Scale and method change labor, stage occupancy, storage and repair needs.

Hybrid: assign each scene/element a method, then connect plate capture, reference, assets, tracking, animation and composite reviews. Charge shared work once. Pure digital animation does not require buying a physical camera.

Research exact technical assumptions from appropriate primary/professional sources before coding. These are planning categories, not claims that every production follows one rigid order. Allow parallel work when prerequisites/resources permit. Revisions reopen only affected tasks; sunk costs and approved versions remain traceable.

## Player decisions and delegation

Coverage, movement, location/stage, light, recorded sound, effects, animation technique, asset reuse, voice/performance, edit and finish choices have requirement previews. The player can choose a source, approve the brief, cast, and delegate detailed execution inside mandates. Auto-planning must still reserve people/equipment, create work, incur costs and respect dependencies; it must not grant instant completion.

Short productions can have small crews and player-operated equipment. Multi-role work consumes time and cannot overlap incompatibly. Larger projects create more work from scope, locations, cast, runtime and method rather than one arbitrary feature-film multiplier. Resource-saving changes are proposals for the player, not silent story edits.

## Starting finances and family

Creation separates family financial circumstances, actual support offered and the player's accessible cash. Options: no contribution, a specified gift, loan with repayment, investment with participation, or access to defined family-owned equipment/resources. Optional family company means explicit ownership and access terms, not universal wealth or unrestricted company cash.

Preview opening balances, obligations and resource access before creation. Family background does not determine ethnicity, personality, craft talent or guaranteed connections. Personal/project/company accounts remain separate with explicit transfers. Loans are not earnings, gifts are not ticket sales, gross is not profit. Migrate existing saves without unexplained new money. Production accommodation is allowed; no unrelated personal housing/tax system.

Playable loop: available funds -> affordable brief -> finance plan -> actual purchases/rentals/work -> delivered project -> supported distribution or paid-job contract -> receipts/obligations -> net funds -> reinvestment. Failure and losses remain possible. Small sustainable careers are valid; no automatic paycheck for merely finishing a film.

## Shop, rentals and inventory

Research useful equipment across budgets from manufacturer specifications and dated price sources, not just flagships: cameras, lenses, audio, lighting, supports, media/storage, power and method-specific capture/workstation/render resources. No invented real manufacturer specification or private rental quote. Store verified price date/currency and separate simulated marketplace pricing.

Loop: compare -> compatibility check -> buy/rent/reserve -> assign project/day -> prepare/test -> use -> maintain/repair -> return/resell/store. Track ownership, cost payer, available dates, condition where modeled, accessories, terms, media/power needs and compatible combinations. One item cannot serve simultaneous shoots. Owned equipment must not incur invented rental charges; rental extensions, late returns and damage need explicit terms.

Only model specifications with genuine consumers. Relevant recording modes, mounts, low-light limitations, stabilization, storage, power and workflow affect capability/constraints, not brand prestige bonuses. An expensive camera cannot fix weak coverage or unavailable sound. Propose alternatives when a combination fails.

## Close known integration gaps

Use each shooting place's working calendar, local time, daylight, weather, modeled applicable child rules and permit preparation, with researched/unknown distinctions. Add unit travel and rest to the schedule, shared availability, fallback locations and coverage notes for sparse locations. Changing locations preserves completed expenses and updates affected work only.

Both studio and distributor release plans support staggered territories, localized deliverables and associated costs. Puppet face methods, painted backgrounds, series bibles and standing sets must be actual work/assets/records when applicable. No decorative hires or unused input fields.
