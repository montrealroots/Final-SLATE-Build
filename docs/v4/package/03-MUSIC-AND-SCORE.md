# Songs, Artists, Composers And Delegated Music

## Three related paths

Support original commissioned score, existing recorded songs, and original commissioned songs; a project can combine them. Distinguish composer, songwriter, recording artist, performer, producer, arranger, orchestrator, music editor and supervisor where modeled. Do not assign these jobs interchangeably based on fame.

Real song/artist/composer records may be researched/imported with stable identities, release dates, credits, duration, version/recording and provenance. A catalog entry does not include audio playback rights, grant permission to synchronize music, or prove a person is available. Do not download or redistribute songs/lyrics by default. Use metadata and original cue descriptions; playback requires appropriately authorized assets. No invented fixed real-artist pay rate. Verified publicly disclosed fees, when available, need date, scope and source; otherwise use clearly simulated negotiated quotes, never “their real rate.”

Before implementing legal/contract detail verify appropriate current official/industry sources. Distinguish the musical composition from the sound recording, and which permissions an intended use requires. Unknown rights holder, unavailable recording or unresolved scope stays explicit. In-game negotiations confer no real-world rights and never contact an actual artist.

## Project-specific music brief

Derive a proposed brief from tone, story period, region, emotional development, pacing, scene lengths, dialogue density, medium and budget. Offer restraint/silence as a valid choice. Period settings may intentionally use anachronistic music if the player chooses; story date and recording availability are different constraints. A song not released by the production date is not silently purchasable.

Provide a cue plan with cue ID, scene/episode/version, in/out points, duration, dramatic purpose, foreground/background/source music/score, intensity, dialogue interaction, motif, proposed track or commission and clearance state. Avoid guessing a real recording's characteristics as facts; use sourced metadata or explicitly proposed interpretation.

Guide Me can propose the complete cue plan and shortlist composers/supervisors within the approved budget. The player can approve once, change individual choices or delegate routine review. Music work then advances through the actual engine. Inadequate funds, denied requests, changed cuts and material overruns return as meaningful decisions, not repetitive notifications.

## Existing-song lifecycle

Discover -> audition/reference metadata -> choose recording and intended use -> identify needed rights/scope -> fictional request -> quote/counteroffer/refusal -> budget approval -> agreement -> edit placement -> clearance verification -> cue sheet -> final delivery -> statements/renewal/expiry where applicable.

Terms include modeled territories, media/platforms, term, duration and prominence of use, episode/project scope, trailer/promotion use if separately covered, payment schedule and permitted versions. Do not assume one song purchase covers a sequel, soundtrack album, trailer or worldwide perpetual use. Replacement should preserve incurred costs and reopen sound/edit tasks where necessary.

A failed request provides routes: another track, different scope, a commissioned piece, authorized library music, a newly recorded version with its own requirements, or silence. Do not let a cover automatically evade underlying rights requirements. Applicable contract checks should be researched rather than invented law.

## Original-score lifecycle

Find composer -> portfolio/fit/availability -> brief -> proposal and fee -> contract -> spotting -> sketches/themes -> player/delegated notes -> revisions -> composition -> arrangement/orchestration -> musicians/studio or electronic production -> recording -> edit/mix -> stems/masters -> approval -> picture-conform changes -> cue sheet/credits -> delivery/payment -> future collaboration.

Tasks depend on method and scope. An electronic score need not rent an orchestra; live sessions need people, space and bookings. Fees reflect simulated scope, deadlines, revision allowances and negotiated services. Name recognition is not an automatic score-quality bonus. The composer can be unavailable or decline; replacements must honor prior work and payments.

Score evaluation considers fit to approved intent, synchronization, dialogue intelligibility, delivery quality and execution. Popular songs are not guaranteed commercial success. The audience engine can respond to modeled fit and promotion without using catalog popularity as a fake causal guarantee.

## Integration invariants

Only cleared/authorized-for-this-simulated-release cues satisfy its delivery gate. A festival cut, theatrical version, streaming version and trailer may have different coverage. Re-editing picture invalidates affected timing/conform work, not every unrelated cue. Music fees post once to the ledger; refunds, cancellation charges and participation follow terms. Credits name actual assigned contributors and chosen recordings, and awards eligibility follows the implemented edition rules rather than assuming any famous song qualifies.

Proof must include one original score, one song request that fails and is replaced, a mixed score/song plan, a changed cut, a territory outside the grant, delegated budget limits, expiry/renewal and save/reload without duplicate payments.
