# Source-First Creation And Individual People

## Create project before creating an empty script

Create Project offers Adapt A Work, Write An Original and Develop A Documentary where supported. Adapt A Work opens the source library first. Browse -> source details -> work/installment -> format and method -> approach -> preview -> create populated project. Cancel leaves no empty project or charge.

Source pages show title, creator, medium, publication date, setting and period, concise premise, central conflict, supported character information, genre, themes, installment relationships and existing adaptations. Include books, games and online stories; maintain at least 500 distinct real source works with per-medium coverage, not duplicate editions counted as works. Prioritize dated bestseller lists and Brandon Sanderson's complete verified bibliography as an explicit research target, with novels, novellas, shorter works, collections and coauthored material distinguished. Record what is covered and missing instead of claiming a complete bibliography from a partial import.

Best-selling needs a named list, metric, year and territory. Source availability follows the game date. Unknown rights remain unknown. Metadata access does not supply a full manuscript. Use concise source-backed descriptions and original adaptation planning; do not reconstruct protected books or game scripts in exhaustive scene-by-scene form. Fuller treatments require suitable public-domain, user-supplied authorized or licensed source content. Never claim an inferred story detail is canon.

## Auto-fill the whole brief

Populate working title, adaptation credit, logline, premise, synopsis, conflict, dramatic question, stakes, themes, tone, pacing, primary/secondary genres, story period, locations, intended audience, target classification, characters, relationships, goals, arcs, structure, major milestones, ending approach and production requirements. For TV populate format, episode length/count, series premise, pilot plan, season scope, episode progression, recurring assets and continuation/finality intent. For documentaries use evidence questions, subjects, access and consent plans; do not fabricate findings or real participation.

Each field records whether it is sourced, a proposed adaptation choice, user-edited or unknown. The UI prioritizes the creative brief, with concise distinctions and details disclosures. Proposed pacing is editable, not a promise of perfect pacing. Match format and resources: a game or long novel may need selection, compression, an ensemble change or multiple seasons. Explain tradeoffs rather than flattening every source to the same template.

Preview all changes; preserve field locks and custom writing; support apply/undo and selective revision. The resulting brief must be structurally complete and internally consistent. If factual knowledge is insufficient, offer explicit original proposals or request material; do not quietly present invention as sourced content. A brief is not a complete screenplay. Writing and production labor still exist but can be delegated in-game.

Provide Guide Me and Manual controls per department. Guide Me proposes a coherent plan inside the approved budget, schedules modeled work, and reports exceptions. It may complete routine in-game actions only within an explicitly approved mandate. Major spending, binding contracts, changed creative intent and overruns return for approval. Never transfer money or contact real people outside the game.

## Characters and originals

Players can create, name, duplicate, edit, reorder, combine, split, archive and restore characters. Stable IDs connect scenes, dialogue, relationships, looks, contracts and credits. Preserve financial and production history on edits. Unknown canon ages stay unknown or become chosen adaptation ages. Support age stages, voice/body/stunt/motion-capture assignments and different performers per episode without duplicate bookings or accidental double pay.

Original writing remains complete, with period and year/range pickers, historical/fictional settings and useful suggestions. Do not constrain genre to an era. Separate story year, visual aesthetic and production date. Film and television classifications are different systems; target audience is different again. Target classification is an intention, not an awarded rating. Build classification consequences from structured content and edits with a researched territory policy.

## People with individual careers

Give fictional people persistent role-specific experience, working preferences, commitments, relationships, negotiation priorities, growth and delivery history. Real people retain sourced biographies and credits separately from this save's invented fees, preferences and behavior. No private traits inferred from names/photos. Dated experience must not count future credits at a historical start.

Full loop: discover -> approach -> available dates -> audition/portfolio/evidence -> shortlist -> terms -> contract -> preparation -> work -> approval/revisions -> payment -> credit -> relationship/reputation -> future opportunity. Include actors, voice performers, screenwriters, directors, producers, casting directors, composers, music supervisors and supported technical specialists. A person's commitments apply across all roles/projects. Include refusal, replacement, withdrawal, late delivery, contract termination and fulfilled obligations.

Player roles remain independent: write/produce/direct without acting, act in another person's film, or take paid writing/directing work. Agents can find actual playable outside jobs, not only financing for the player's projects. Representation needs submissions, conflicts, negotiation, commissions, term, renewal and termination, with obligations retained after a relationship ends.
