# Reputation, opportunity and deal economics

## Separate dimensions
Public recognition; commercial record; creative/critical standing; reliability; relationship with a particular company/person; role-specific experience; genre/format/market relevance. Skills and innate talent are not inferred from these. The player may be an established writer and inexperienced director simultaneously.

## Suggested model contract—not an established real-world equation
For each career event store: event ID, date, project, role, responsibility share, market/format context, observed outcome, expected outcome, evidence confidence and model version. Derive bounded contributions using residual performance relative to documented expectations. Use robust scaling to prevent tiny-budget percentage returns from dominating; cap single-project influence; diminish repeated/closely related recognition; retain an established career baseline with recency weighting.

Weights and thresholds must live in versioned calibration data, not scattered magic numbers. Candidate aggregation: weighted sum of bounded event contributions plus an experience/confidence measure. Do not publish arbitrary numerical weights as industry facts. Calibrate against reproducible synthetic careers: novice, reliable independent, critically strong niche creator, commercial hitmaker and established multi-role career. Show events contributing to a grade and the consequence of the next achievement. Low evidence means uncertain standing, not proven incompetence.

UI grade labels can be Emerging, Established, In Demand and Leading within the relevant market/role. Choose boundaries after calibration, expose why the grade applies, and avoid an automatic global status. Nomination, win, delivery and commercial result are distinct events but must not multiply the same accomplishment without bounds. Reviews, audience satisfaction and finance are separate evidence streams.

## Company choice process
1. Is the project eligible for this company's medium, territory and current mandate?
2. Does the company have budget, schedule and distribution capacity?
3. How does the project fit its recorded and simulated strategy?
4. Does the player's relevant track record reduce uncertainty?
5. What are projected returns, commitments and relationship risks?
6. Offer, counter, request changes, defer or decline with concrete reasons.

Reputation affects access, bargaining range and perceived risk, not a guaranteed green light. Seeded decision variation should reflect uncertainty and not reroll on every click. Preserve a decision's inputs so the player can understand it.

## First-look lifecycle
Eligible evidence → dated offer → scope/term preview → negotiate → accept → reserve exclusivity → overhead installments → covered project submission → consideration deadline → accept/pass → financing or turnaround → development recoupment → expiration/renewal/termination.

Film and television scope are separate; covered project types and existing commitments are explicit. First-look is not first refusal, overall services exclusivity or distribution. Implement only a type whose obligations work. Payments distinguish overhead, development advances, production funding and personal compensation. Terms in a real studio's simulated offer are not claimed real contractual terms.

A lucrative offer is justified by leverage, expected output and company capacity. Do not scale overhead indefinitely with fame. Negotiation can trade shorter term, larger fund, narrower exclusivity, more compensation or faster consideration. The same upside may impose obligations. Owner can reject; no auto-acceptance or automatic spending.

Expiration stops future overhead but preserves existing project obligations where modeled. Cancellation of a project is not cancellation of an entire agreement. Another buyer's funding cannot repay a development advance twice. Withdrawal cannot bypass consideration windows. Renegotiation and renewal require a new explicit agreement.

## Real destinations
Use identity records for real studios, networks, services, aggregators and exhibitors; verify public route requirements before modeling them. Some accept direct submissions, some use intermediaries, some offer self-service distribution. Unknown policies cannot become guaranteed access. Country availability and historical dates matter. Rates without public evidence are calibrated simulation values with ranges. Real names must not conceal generic identical behavior.

## Money invariants
Committed funds reduce availability but are not the same as spent cash. Reservations expire or release on cancellation. Receivables do not become cash until settlement. A studio-funded production budget is not the player's paycheck. Repayment follows explicit priority and caps. Company-to-player transfers post once to each account. Rounding and currency conversion use deterministic policies. Bankrupt or unfunded actions cannot quietly create money; recovery actions are visible.
