# Full gameplay loops: features within features

The following loops share entities, scheduling and accounting. They must not become isolated minigames. Each loop includes a consequence beyond its own screen.

## 1. Identity, starting circumstances and family support
Choose identity, birthday, languages, starting city/date and background → preview initial funds and obligations → confirm → create opening ledger entries → begin career. Background can supply a gift, loan, equipment access or contacts, each explicitly defined. A loan has repayment; a gift does not quietly acquire interest. Family wealth must not grant creative competence. Keep company and personal cash distinct and explain the origin of every opening balance. User can inspect funds before committing.

## 2. Discover and adapt a source
Browse/search by medium, genre, era, setting and available story detail → inspect a concise source overview → choose Adapt This Work → select feature/short/series/special and production method → preview a complete editable proposal → apply or cancel → revise without losing provenance.

Proposal includes concept, original concise logline, premise, central conflict, themes, tone, proposed pacing, audience intent, era, setting, characters, relationships and suitable format structure. Source facts and adaptation decisions have separate provenance. Do not copy a book or full script as an autofill mechanism. A target rating is intent, not a guarantee of classification.

A source may support several interpretations; changing viewpoint, medium or scope must propagate to characters, sequence/episode structure, workload and estimated budget. Preserve the user's edits during refresh; offer a field-level diff. Undo must restore the prior brief and downstream uncommitted choices. Once production is committed, revisions go through change control rather than silently rebuilding everything.

## 3. Characters and casting requirements
Define any number of source-supported or original characters, including one or none where appropriate → choose narrative function and billing separately → age/era/language/performance needs → scenes/episodes → casting requirements. Protagonist, antagonist and highest billing are not synonyms. Support ensemble, co-leads, unnamed characters, creatures, voice and performance capture.

Aliases map to one character identity; age stages can have multiple performers; doubles and voice performers are separate assignments. Role changes recalculate affected scenes, bookings, wardrobe and credits. Never force “The Rival” to satisfy a minimum cast size. Genre and ethnicity do not dictate moral role.

## 4. Original writing remains available
Keep manual original scripts, retire obsolete generated-story quota requirements. Era/year pickers include historical, present, future and invented settings; exact year is optional where the story does not need one. Genre combinations inform suggested structures but do not impose identical plots. Suggestions are editable alternatives, not random text pretending to be authored depth.

## 5. Screenwriting and revision
Outline → sequence/episode plan → scenes → draft → feedback → revision → production draft. Each scene records purpose, location, time, characters, performance and production demands. Structure permits nonlinear chronology, anthology, silent work and documentary approaches. Version drafts; retain notes and resolved/unresolved issues. Script revision changes time and work scope. Writing help requires an accepted engagement; a declined co-writer offer must return a clear decision and recovery path.

For series: season engine, episode arcs, recurring sets/cast, serialized/episodic structure, continuity and season ending. A season is not a feature with a multiplied runtime. Renewed seasons inherit contracts and unresolved story/production commitments where appropriate.

## 6. Packaging and feasibility
Script → breakdown → cast/crew needs → locations/assets/equipment → preliminary schedule → budget → funding plan → feasibility decision. Identify the actual bottleneck: missing lead, unavailable set, unaffordable kit, incomplete animation assets or distribution constraints. Explain alternatives and consequences. Do not declare greenlit because a generic progress bar reached 100%.

## 7. Hiring people
Search real/simulated eligible people → inspect relevant credits and simulated availability → audition/read/portfolio review as appropriate → shortlist → offer → negotiate/accept/decline → reserve dates → work → approval/payment/credit → future relationship. Separate agent, casting director, line producer, department lead and performer responsibilities. A famous name can expand access or audience interest without guaranteeing fit or quality. Offers expire and overlapping commitments produce actual conflicts.

Player can fill multiple roles, but overlapping work consumes finite time. Hiring delegates specific tasks and decision authority. Collaborator rejection, illness/unavailability and replacement must preserve completed work and expose costs. Repeated failed offers must not debit accepted fees.

## 8. Equipment and facilities
Browse verified equipment metadata → select rent/buy/access → reserve dates → pay deposit/price → receive → use → return/maintain/resell where supported. Model camera compatibility, lenses, sound, storage, lighting and workflow constraints only where they affect a decision. Do not invent manufacturer specifications. Expensive cameras cannot rescue a poor script by a flat score bonus. A missing required component must block or offer an explicit alternative, never secretly purchase it.

Facilities include stages, recording rooms, edit suites, render resources and stop-motion workspaces. Equipment and facility bookings share the schedule. Reuse owned assets across projects with condition and availability, rather than repurchasing them invisibly.

## 9. Live-action production
Break down scenes → scout/secure locations → blocking/rehearsal → shot plan → book cast/crew/kit → daily call plan → shoot coverage → review dailies → select takes → identify pickups → wrap. Weather and daylight affect exterior work; travel and setup consume time; continuity links wardrobe, props and scene chronology. Additional takes trade time/cost against specific problems; unlimited takes cannot guarantee perfection.

## 10. 2D animation
Design/storyboards → animatic → voice timing → layout → animation → cleanup/inbetween/color → compositing → review. Late timing revisions can invalidate animation; fixing an animatic early costs less than reworking finished sequences. Reusable assets reduce some work, not all creative demands.

## 11. 3D animation
Design → model → texture/look development → rig → layout → animation → simulation → lighting → render → composite. Rig issues constrain motion; render capacity and shot complexity affect scheduling. Approvals freeze versions and downstream changes create targeted rework. Do not use live-action shooting days for every animation task.

## 12. Stop motion
Design → puppet/set fabrication → armature/testing → lighting/camera lock → capture → replacement/repair → cleanup/composite → edit. Physical continuity, puppet wear, replacement parts and stage occupancy have consequences. Capture sessions consume actual work units; interruptions can require setup restoration. The player can choose simpler staging and limited movement with visible artistic tradeoffs.

## 13. Hybrid, VFX and performance capture
Split shots by method → shared shot IDs/versions → live-action plates or capture → tracking/cleanup/retargeting → animation/effects → integration → review. Voice, body capture, face capture and screen acting are distinct credits and bookings. Changing an approved take after cleanup produces a confirmation with rework scope, money and schedule effects.

## 14. Documentary and nonfiction
Research/access → contributor engagement → interviews/observational capture/archive plan → logging/transcription → story assembly → factual review → edit. Do not force scripted protagonists or invented factual claims. Contributor withdrawal and missing access create concrete alternatives. Real source facts and simulated production events remain distinguishable.

## 15. Short films and specials
Use their real smaller scope without assuming a short is a failed feature. Match crew scale, festival/digital routes, sound/delivery requirements and likely earning routes. A profitable short can improve reliability and relevant reputation without automatically earning blockbuster leverage.

## 16. Design departments
Era/setting/character needs → researched design options → materials/assets → fitting/build/rehearsal → continuity → on-set use → revisions → archived inventory. Costume, makeup, hair, prosthetics, props and sets each have distinct work and dependencies. An “1980s zombie” example is a combination, not a universal template. Tropical contemporary romance and historical court drama must produce different requirements without hardcoding one story per genre.

## 17. Music and sound
Spotting brief → score/song choices → composer/artist or recording selection → proposed engagement/use → schedule and cost → demo/cue → revision → recording/edit → mix → approved cue sheet → delivery and credits. Retain cue duration, scene purpose, instrumental/vocal needs and version. Licensed recording, new recording, original composition and temp track are different assets. No audible copyrighted music is bundled merely because its title exists in a catalog.

Track any supported permissions/clearances separately from simulated fees; follow the owner's latest gameplay policy. Public price evidence is optional; confidential rates must be modeled and labeled, not invented as fact. Temp music cannot silently become a cleared final soundtrack. Mixing, dialogue clarity, ambience and effects affect intelligibility and deliverables rather than a generic “sound +5”.

## 18. Editing and finishing
Ingest → assembly → rough cut → feedback → revised cut → picture lock → sound/color/VFX → captions/subtitles/audio-description where required → masters → QC → delivery. Missing shots or incompatible assets require pickups, re-edit or explicit compromise. Cutting a scene after work exists triggers cost/continuity review. Reopening picture lock invalidates only dependent tasks, with a clear change list.

## 19. Finance and company operations
Personal support/earned income → company or project contribution → budget categories → commitments/reserves → invoiced work → payment → remaining availability → release receipts → contractual recoupment → distributable proceeds → personal withdrawal. Transfers balance on both sides and are idempotent. Debt is not income; revenue is not profit; profit is not immediately withdrawable cash. Forecasts, earned amounts and settled cash remain distinct.

Fundraising can fail or be partial. A project may scale down, reschedule, seek partners or stop, with sunk costs preserved. Company overhead has dates and a source of funds. Avoid unsolicited tax/legal expansion; model only required gameplay obligations.

## 20. Distribution, publicity and release
Delivery → route/territory/window selection → buyer proposal or self-service booking → negotiation → material acceptance → campaign → release → report → settlement → later windows → catalog life. Publicity choices consume budget and reach an audience, not guaranteed sales. Reviews, word of mouth and competing releases affect subsequent demand. Long-tail licensing, rerelease and later seasons reuse the project identity.

Release plans display why a destination is available and what it requires. A selective buyer cannot be reached by guaranteed upload. Festival hold and exclusivity constrain dates. A release date change propagates to marketing, screenings, awards and payments. Show the player's best next action when a route fails.

## 21. Career, outside work and relationships
Build evidence → receive/find opportunities → negotiate responsibility/rate/dates → accept → complete work → receive credit/pay → update relationship and relevant reputation → new opportunities. Real studio participation is a simulated relationship, not an endorsement. People remember defined professional events, not arbitrary hidden moral scores. Delays/cancellations and termination compensation use explicit modeled agreements. Outside jobs cannot silently consume time already promised to the player's production.

## 22. Long-term world and legacy
Weekly releases/audience reports → changing demand/capacity → renewals/cancellations → new commissions → career opportunities → new projects. Historical catalogs provide context and competition; future productions emerge with stable IDs, coherent budgets and finite resources. Records, filmographies and awards update from completed events. Archive old detail without deleting the evidence needed for royalties, credits and save integrity.
