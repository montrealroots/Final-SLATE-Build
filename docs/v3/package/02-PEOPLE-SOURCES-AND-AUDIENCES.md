# People, Adaptations And Industry Outcomes

## Distinct people and careers

Extend the current real-person hiring system and fictional population. Each person has persistent professional history, role-specific skills, working methods, project preferences, development goals, availability, commitments, negotiation terms, relationships and reputation dimensions. Do not reduce everyone to one quality number or one random trait.

For real people, sourced facts and game-authored attributes are separate. Simulated willingness, fees, tastes, skills and interpersonal behavior must be presented as this save's fiction, never as verified personal facts. No private medical, religious or intimate attributes inferred from images or names. Career evidence must be dated; future credits relative to the game's date must not silently grant present experience. Offer an explicit dated-world snapshot policy for historical starts and explain uncertainty.

Distinctiveness must affect decisions: matching role needs, reading/audition evidence, preparation, preferred material, scheduling, contract terms, delivery and future collaboration. It must not guarantee outcomes. People learn from completed work and receive credible career opportunities. Availability, travel, rest and commitments are shared across all projects and jobs. Preserve history on pack refresh/removal.

Complete loops for actors/actresses, voice performers, screenwriters, directors, producers, casting directors and supported department specialists. Existing paid writer/director paths are retained. Add producer and casting-director responsibilities before allowing those hires: scoped mandate, delegated tasks, costs, decisions requiring player approval, accountability, credit and termination. Hiring never removes the player's creative control. Representation requires actual agent search, mandate, submissions, negotiation, commission accounting, conflicts, renewal and termination—not a decorative agent name.

## Source-material catalog: 500+ records

Support novels, short stories, comics/graphic novels, plays, video games, online fiction/web serials, and other verified media. Distinguish works from editions, translations, franchises and individual installments. Record creator, publication date, medium, genres with rationale, synopsis source, canonical link, provenance, rights status/unknown status, relevant territory and version. Do not infer adaptation rights from availability online, TMDB inclusion or noncommercial use.

The 500 records are genuinely distinct works; editions do not inflate the quota. Require representation from books, games and online stories plus a published coverage matrix for the full source catalog. Avoid invented source titles masquerading as real works. Fictional in-world source works can supplement this catalog but must be counted separately.

Loop: discover/read -> evaluate adaptation approach -> check rights/access -> simulated or documented option agreement -> development brief -> screenplay -> approvals and revisions -> exercise/renew/expire option -> production -> agreed credit and compensation -> release -> reporting and future rights. An in-game rights deal is fictional and conveys no real-world permission. Model chain-of-title status, expiry, territory, media, term, scope, credit and payments without pretending to provide legal advice.

Use original and appropriately authorized/public-domain material for fully populated reusable adaptation blueprints. Unknown/protected records can remain reference-only until an appropriate playable treatment is supplied. Track blocked enrichment honestly without disabling original creation. Do not replace the requested count with 500 duplicated summaries.

## Box office and history books

Keep recorded historical results distinct from simulated release results and scenario projections. Each observation needs title ID, territory, currency, gross type, period, as-of date, admissions if available, revision status and source. Domestic, international and worldwide totals cannot be added as if disjoint. Gross is not profit, distributor revenue or player earnings.

Simulated loop: booking and screen availability -> release date/territory -> publicity and competition -> attendance -> ticket sales -> exhibitor/distributor allocation -> contract recoupment and participant payments -> ledger -> revised bookings/holdovers -> later windows -> record-book eligibility. Use accounting invariants and explain each earnings component. No invented receipts labeled as real box office.

History books distinguish opening weekend, weekly, annual and lifetime; territory; nominal and explicitly adjusted currency; actual and simulated records. Define tie handling, eligibility, rereleases and late corrections. Records update once per result revision and link back to the project and underlying observations. A historical import refresh must not rewrite a career's already-settled earnings.

## Weekly TV and streaming audiences

Do not conflate viewership, household rating, demographic rating, share, hours watched, views, completion and ranking. Every observation carries metric definition, territory, reporting window, platform/network, episode/season/title scope, live/delayed window where applicable, source and as-of date. Missing figures remain unknown. A ranking cannot become a fabricated audience count.

Provide weekly in-game reports even when no real observation exists: simulated values must be clearly marked and follow the same units. Historical weekly data is imported only where actually published and available under suitable access terms. No requirement to invent unpublished ratings to fill a week. Real weekly updates are an explicit supported sync operation with last-updated status, not a promise that a closed game keeps fetching.

Simulation loop: schedule/availability -> reach and promotion -> audience choice and competition -> viewing/retention -> episode and season report -> sponsor/license receipts where contracted -> renewal, cancellation, rescheduling or later windows -> new production commitments. Linear, streaming batch, streaming weekly, daily shows, live broadcasts and limited series need distinct scheduling and audience behavior. No universal conversion from streaming views to TV rating points.

All financial or creative feedback must connect to modeled decisions and show uncertainty. More episodes or more marketing does not guarantee success. Show active shows and upcoming releases in a dated world calendar; frozen save inputs prevent refreshed catalog data changing prior outcomes.

## More companies

Keep existing Disney, NBCUniversal, Paramount/Warner-related and Sony records; verify dated corporate relationships instead of assuming permanent ownership. Add international and independent companies in evidence-backed batches with country, active dates, labels, production/distribution/network roles, credits and sources. Catalog completeness is scoped to checked discovery queries, not asserted as every work ever made.

Each playable company needs operating capacity, slate strategy, submissions, decisions, financing/distribution terms, deadlines, deliverables, payment obligations, release behavior and future relationships. Real executives remain factual references unless explicitly modeled as fictional behavior. Do not infer personal tastes from job titles. Different companies must produce testably different decisions for appropriate scenarios.
