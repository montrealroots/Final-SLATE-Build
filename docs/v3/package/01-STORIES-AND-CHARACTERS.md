# Complete Creative Blueprints And Editable Characters

## 1. What choosing an idea does

A player can choose a complete blueprint, choose individual suggestions, shuffle only selected unlocked fields, or write everything. Show title, primary/secondary genres, format, tone, era, setting, concise premise and origin before selection. Preview all changed fields. Preserve locked fields and custom writing. Apply the accepted patch atomically with undo; never silently overwrite a project.

Every full blueprint supplies: working title, logline, premise, synopsis, central dramatic question, themes, audience intention, tone, story year/period, geographic or fictional setting, narrative structure, named characters, character goals and conflicts, relationships, arcs, stakes, major reversals, ending, locations, and a coherent scene outline. Include visual and sound suggestions separately from required production choices. Names can be changed without destroying links.

Film blueprints include an opening, development, escalation, climax and resolution; structures other than three acts are supported. TV blueprints include format and episode length, series premise, repeatable episode engine, pilot outline, complete first-season arc, episode-by-episode progression, character changes, recurring locations, and continuation or final-season logic. A limited series must not acquire endless renewal by default. Documentary blueprints use subjects, access plans, questions, evidence and ethical/consent tasks rather than fabricated findings or guaranteed real-person participation.

Every structured field supported by the game must either be populated consistently or marked explicitly not applicable. A cinematic treatment is not a fully written screenplay: label draft level truthfully. Blueprint selection creates material to develop; it does not skip writing labor, financing, casting, permissions or production.

## 2. Library breadth and originality

Author the quota in 00-START-HERE.md. For each genre, map premise patterns, conflicts, story forms, tones, periods, settings, protagonist relationships and endings. Vary these intentionally. Renaming people, changing country, substituting a monster or paraphrasing a logline does not create a new quota-eligible story.

Maintain a diversity matrix by genre, format, protagonist goal, opposition, central dilemma, structure, setting type, period and resolution. Flag exact and near duplicates within and across batches. Automated similarity checks assist editorial inspection; do not claim they prove originality. Review every blueprint for internal coherence and duplicates, and retain review status. Count authored entries, parameter combinations and reviewed entries separately.

Create broad international settings without turning countries, ethnicities or languages into personality or quality stereotypes. Distinguish story setting from filming place; research real cultural or historical claims. Fictional locations must be labeled. Accessibility needs and demographics are not automatic artistic penalties or bonuses.

The 149 genres need their own useful suggestions; four identical routes offered across a whole family do not satisfy this. Mixed genres must integrate both into the central conflict, stakes and resolution, not merely add a secondary tag. Preserve unusual combinations; explain constraints and allow creative overrides where sensible.

## 3. Origins: 'Which one is it from?'

Every option has originType: original, licensed-adaptation, public-domain-adaptation, user-supplied, or reference-only. Show an original blueprint's own title and identifier. For real sources show source title, creator, medium, edition/version and link. For a real film/TV reference show the recorded title, release year and stable catalog ID. Never label a newly generated plot as originating from a real work.

Do not copy full third-party scripts, plots or character sets into the original library. A reference catalog entry is not automatically an authorized adaptation. Keep source provenance behind a concise player-facing origin label with a details disclosure.

## 4. Characters must be fully editable

Provide Add Character, Rename, Duplicate, Archive/Remove, Restore and reorder, before and after applying a blueprint. A story character is separate from the actor who portrays them. Cast changes do not rename characters; character changes do not rewrite a real person's biography.

Character data: stable ID, name, aliases, pronouns, story age/range, character form (human, creature, object, abstract or custom), narrative function, billing prominence, goals, motivations, obstacles, contradictions, relationships, arc, voice/dialogue brief, appearance/look requirements, costume states, makeup states, scene participation and episode continuity. Protagonist is not synonymous with lead billing. Ensemble, multiple protagonists, antagonists without villains and nonhuman stories must work.

Allow multiple performers per character (age stages, voice, body, motion capture, stunt double), multiple characters per performer, and different performers across episodes with explicit continuity. Every assignment has method, scope and credit rules. Do not double-book a multi-role person or pay twice for one contracted obligation.

Deleting a referenced character shows affected scenes, relationships, offers, contracts, looks and credits. Offer reassignment or archival; never silently delete financial history. A rename follows stable references everywhere and survives reload. Relationship edits update both sides without accidental duplicate bonds. Character arcs affect structured continuity assessments; arbitrary prose is not secretly scored.

## 5. Connect story decisions to work

Use typed requirements, not keyword guesses: crowds, stunts, intimacy coordination, creatures, practical effects, VFX, animation methods, period wardrobe, multilingual dialogue, night exteriors, music, animals and locations. Each selected requirement feeds task dependencies, specialists, schedules, equipment, costs, revisions and deliverables as applicable.

No automatic quality bonus for selecting expensive craft or a fashionable genre. Outcomes depend on preparation, suitability, execution, constraints and seeded uncertainty. Creative preference remains subjective. Show useful consequences before accepting changes, then actual ledger and schedule consequences after.

Changing a story after work begins must create scoped revision/change orders with approval, sunk costs retained, affected work reopened and unaffected work preserved. A removed need cannot refund completed work automatically. Track draft and production versions through distribution, eligibility and career credits.
