# Real People As Playable Participants

## Required outcome
Imported people can participate in the player's projects through the existing casting/hiring systems. A person page offers applicable actions: Consider For A Role, Invite To Read, Offer A Role, Approach As Screenwriter, Approach As Director, Approach As Producer, or an appropriate crew job. Actions explain unmet requirements. Do not simply rename existing fictional NPCs while leaving duplicate availability and histories.

## Separate evidence from simulation
PersonIdentity: global ID, sourced names, known birth/death dates, credit history, portrait references, source timestamps.
CareerEvidence: dated credits by actual job and medium. A crew credit as producer does not prove ownership or personal funding; executive producer is not interchangeable with line producer.
SimulationProfile: role eligibility model, availability calendar, fee expectations, career interests, project-fit preferences, representation route and relationship state. Every inferred or generated field is explicitly simulation, not a statement about the real person.

Do not infer ethnicity, religion, sexuality, health, private contacts or personality from names/photos. Do not assign real talent rankings as verified facts or turn popularity/vote counts into ability. Use transparent calibrated game capabilities where needed, with dated professional experience as one limited input, not proof of mastery. No negative character labels such as unreliable or difficult as alleged real facts. Relationship changes describe events in this save only.

Unknown age must remain unknown. Before age-sensitive employment rules apply, require a usable modeled age with an explicit simulation label or keep that hiring route unavailable with a reason; never silently assume adult. Known deceased people cannot accept new work after their death. Historical reference pages remain accessible. Foreign-language proficiency, accent capability and travel origin must not be guessed from nationality; use sourced professional evidence or a clearly simulated audition result.

## Identity resolution and pool size
Merge imports by provider ID, preserve aliases, verify ambiguous cross-provider matches and keep pending matches separate. The same person in Disney, Universal and Sony records must have one schedule and one contract ledger. Real people should not be silently capped to the first 50 famous names. Use indexed search, role/job facets, pagination and lazy simulation activation. Instantiate detailed mutable state when shortlisted, approached or involved; keep background profiles lightweight. New pack installation must not duplicate active people or reset relationships.

Preserve the existing seed-driven fictional workforce and use it where evidence/data is absent. Filtering must tell users whether a person lacks details, is unavailable, or simply has no recorded evidence for that role. Unknowns are not negative ability scores. Keep career and event generation computationally bounded.

## Actor/actress loop
Write role → define billing, work type, languages, ages and dates → find candidates → shortlist → availability inquiry → audition/self-tape offer → acceptance/refusal → material and deadline → performance evaluation → callback/chemistry read → offer → negotiation → contract → fitting/rehearsal → shoot/voice/capture → pickup/replacement if necessary → pay → credit → release → relationship/next opportunity.

Include human, creature, voice, motion capture, performance capture, puppetry and stunt-performance pathways with appropriate tasks. A voice role does not acquire on-set physical requirements automatically. Motion capture does not automatically count as voice acting. Children trigger the researched or explicitly modeled local rules.

The player can cast themselves into one or several allowed roles, decline to act, or work only as writer/producer/director. Their own schedule and work requirements still matter. One actor can play multiple characters only with a feasible production method and schedule. Double casting is a choice with makeup, camera, stand-in or post implications, not necessarily forbidden.

Auditions vary by specific material, role demands, preparation, modeled skill, chemistry and uncertainty. Results give actionable observations about this simulated read, not universal judgments about a real person. Repeating the exact read must not reroll endlessly for a perfect result; additional preparation or new material has a dated action and cost.

## Crew and creative leadership loop
Project brief → breakdown → required job → candidates with job-specific evidence → availability → approach → scope and deliverables → negotiation → contract → work milestones → review/notes → accepted revisions → payment → credit → future collaboration.

Support writer, co-writer, episode writer, story editor, director, episode director, producer, line producer and existing specialist crew roles according to the game's job taxonomy. A person may hold multiple jobs, but time and compensation must be reconciled. Do not multiply capability merely because someone has more titles. Producers contribute modeled work/relationships/oversight; do not fabricate access to the person's real fortune.

## Availability and contracts
Availability is date/role/location specific: inquiry holds, confirmed bookings, travel, rehearsal, shoot, capture, voice, ADR and pickups. External real schedules are usually incomplete; do not advertise invented calendars as factual. Simulated other work must respect already known commitments and game-generated contracts. Test conflicts across concurrent projects and companies.

An offer has project/person/role IDs, dates, work units, compensation schedule, credits, option terms where modeled, expiry and version. Preview costs and uncertainty before sending. Replies take game time. Acceptance reserves resources and money; cancellation/replacement follows agreed terms. Counteroffers cannot duplicate deposits or erase earlier obligations. Availability can be conditional pending dates or renegotiation.

Real-world fees must not be fabricated. Calibrated simulation fees are allowed, visibly described as game estimates; sourced published figures need currency, date and scope. Budget constraints should support lower-cost candidates, fewer workdays, scope changes or postponement, without making every desired star accessible on day one.

## Work, credits and downstream behavior
Every contract connects to actual tasks and work records. Performance contributions feed the appropriate production/quality model; all credit eligibility derives from contribution and agreement. Replaced performers keep credits/pay where their work remains under existing rules. Departed crew keep earned payments and applicable credits. Series contracts distinguish episode commitments, recurring roles, regular roles and options; returning regulars cannot magically satisfy conflicting dates.

On release, link this save's simulated filmography to the real person's profile in a separate section. It never modifies historical TMDB records. Show the player's project's actual role/billing, episodes and dates. Awards and reputation read those records. A good collaboration can affect future willingness; a failed project does not automatically become a personal grievance. Agents/representatives, where modeled, are simulated routing rather than real contact services. Never send messages to actual people.

## Essential proof
One real-person fixture appears under two studios → one profile → audition → negotiated contract → rehearsal → shoot → delivery → paid ledger → on-screen credit → filmography → save/reload. Attempt a conflicting booking in another project and observe a real refusal or rescheduling. Repeat for a writer/director hire and for voice/capture. Demonstrate cancellation, replacement, unavailable dates and missing age. Live TMDB smoke tests verify IDs; deterministic fixtures verify behavior without asserting invented personal facts.
