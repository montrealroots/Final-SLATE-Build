# Creative Choices With Consequences

## Breadth with integrity
Provide large libraries for broad creative categories and practical lists for narrow ones. Initial targets are design goals, not claims that the attached starter set already meets them:
- Logline/story-premise builder: at least 1,000 validated distinct composed suggestions across the catalog; prioritize coherent concepts rather than raw Cartesian products.
- Themes, conflicts, character goals, settings, relationship dynamics and plot structures: hundreds of curated building blocks across families, expanding through coherent combinations.
- Genre treatments: every existing canonical genre has a definition, applicable subtypes, relevant premise/setting/production suggestions and at least several meaningful routes where appropriate. No arbitrary requirement for 1,000 subgenres.
- Costume, makeup, production design, effects, cinematography, sound and editing: extensive libraries keyed by medium, era, place, culture, character and scene demand. Report authored base entries and derived combinations separately.
- Exact years, aspect ratios, frame rates and real countries: bounded accurate lists, not padded catalogs.

Maintain counts for authored options, templates, unique normalized combinations, tested combinations and empty/underrepresented categories. Reject duplicates that only swap adjectives. Vary conflicts, agency, scale, stakes, locations and ending logic. Provide multiple cultures and regions without treating a country as one stereotype. Cultural authenticity is not derived from ethnicity or rewarded with biological ability bonuses.

## The option record
Every mechanical option needs: stable ID; version; field; label; original description; provenance (original authored, sourced fact, simulation); tags; requirements; incompatibilities; typed project patch; effect bindings; units; scope; preview explanation; source references where factual; test IDs. Descriptive choices record their document/UI consumer rather than pretending to influence arbitrary scores. JSON Schema is included; adapt effects to actual typed commands, never execute code embedded in data packs.

Bindings specify existing engine function and output variable. Category-level bindings are acceptable when every option parameterizes them meaningfully. Do not claim a thousand bespoke engines when a shared task model reads a thousand records. Reject missing bindings, dangling references, cyclic dependencies and unsupported units during build.

## Screenwriting: selectable starting points to working script
Project family → medium → format → primary and secondary genres → subtypes → tone → themes → period and region → protagonist goal → obstacle → stakes → hook → premise/logline → synopsis → treatment → outline → scenes → dialogue/voice notes → revisions → table read → production breakdown.

Each step may be entered manually or selected and edited. Show the full logline before Apply. The resulting text references actual named characters and settings. Template placeholders cannot leak into saved prose. Multiple leads and ensemble stories must be supported. Free-text edits mark the generated wording as customized; they do not secretly change structured stakes or effects.

Theme selections propose conflicts and scene beats, not guaranteed quality. Social satire can suggest institutions, conflicting incentives and ironic reversals. Slapstick can propose physical business whose selected scenes require rehearsal, props and safety. Horror-comedy can create tonal targets; whether the finished film meets them depends on scene mix, performance and edit choices.

Genre mixing uses primary intent and secondary flavor. Propose optional blends without erasing genre identity. Distinguish genre, plot trope, tone, setting, visual style, format, medium and financing scale. Stop-motion is a technique; indie is a financing/production context; neither substitutes for a genre.

Characters: name suggestions plus unrestricted custom names; age/range; pronouns; role size; narrative function; species/body; motivation; flaw; relationship; arc; accent/language; physical action; costume states; makeup states; dialogue scenes; screen time. A villain is not automatically supporting, and a co-lead is not automatically eligible for a specific award. Keep narrative function, billing, contract and award placement separate.

Scenes: purpose, participants, story location, physical shoot location, time of day, interiors/exteriors, emotional turn, dialogue/action emphasis, props, extras, stunts, water/animals/crowds, makeup state and effects method. A compact scene card expands into breakdown details. Plot conflict does not itself reserve an expensive shoot; only applied scene requirements do.

## Era and style
Three independent controls: story period, aesthetic reference, actual production date. Add a fourth when useful: technology permitted in the fictional world. A film made in 2026, set in 1986, with 1980s-inspired visuals is valid. A contemporary story with retro styling is also valid. Let intentional anachronism be selected and explained rather than categorically forbidden.

Era choices resolve with region, social context, season, occupation and budget. Costume suggestions should not imply everybody in a decade wore the same clothes. Museum collections, period archives and local research support factual references; game workload and costs remain calibrated models. Period vehicles, signage and music are distinct departments, with substitutions and clearance needs.

## Department loops
Costumes: brief → wardrobe by character and scene → source/build/rent → sizes and fittings → movement/camera test → approve → continuity states and duplicates → shoot use → damage/repair/laundry → return/storage → settlement and credits. Rain, stunts and successive dirt/blood states can require duplicates; a simple color choice need not incur a separate fee.

Makeup/hair: look → materials and method → tests and sensitivity constraints → performer approval where needed → prep/removal time → chair and specialist capacity → scene order → continuity/reset → touch-ups → completion. Distinguish costume masks, appliances, digital replacement and puppet finishes. No real person is assigned a medical condition from guesswork.

Production design: world brief → location/stage choice → plans → scale/materials → build → dressing → lighting/camera test → approval → shoot → strike/reuse. Set dimensions and camera coverage affect build scope, not just an automatic price multiplier.

Camera and lighting: framing/movement/coverage → rig and power needs → tests → setup schedule → weather/light windows → takes → editorial coverage → grade. A handheld look does not automatically make quality worse; it creates different execution requirements.

Sound/music: location noise assessment → recording method → dialogue coverage → Foley/ADR → score or source music → rights → mix/version requirements → delivery. A licensed song preset does not grant real rights; the game models a simulated clearance for a fictional use and records its scope.

Editing: narrative priorities → assemblies → pacing and continuity → test screening → notes → revisions → picture lock → sound/VFX/grade conform → versions → delivery. Audience feedback should identify modeled causes without pretending to analyze arbitrary prose.

## Medium-specific production
Live action: cast availability, rehearsals, physical sites, shooting, takes, continuity, practical effects and post.
2D animation: design, boards, animatic, voice, layouts, animation, cleanup, color and composite; reused assets reduce repeated work only where compatible.
3D animation: approved designs, models, topology, rigs, surfacing, layout, performance, simulation, lighting, render capacity and compositing; revised models invalidate dependent work selectively.
Stop-motion: scale, armatures, puppets, replacement faces, miniature sets, camera/lighting stability, animator capacity, frames/shots, repairs and compositing. Do not assign a universal frames-per-day rate as a fact.
Hybrid: allocate technique per shot/asset, define shared references and interface tasks; do not simply add every pipeline in full to every scene.
Documentary: research, contributors, access, consent, field production, records, fact checking, edit and release review; do not force a fictional-script structure.
Unscripted: format, episode structure, participants, rules, location/studio logistics, recorded outcomes and edit; preserve withdrawals and permissions.
Television: series bible, season arc, episode briefs, writers/directors by episode, returning cast, standing sets, rolling production and delivery, continuity and renewal decisions.

## Worked acceptance example: an 1980s-inspired zombie film
Choose a feature, live action, horror primary/comedy secondary, story year 1986 and production year 2026. Choose an original small-town outbreak premise. Set slow reanimated creatures, practical appliances, a limited location cluster, period workwear and a nighttime siege.

Apply creates an editable logline and structured brief; it offers scene beats rather than overwriting scenes. Selected crowd scenes create extras and makeup demand. Appliances create tests, specialist time, reset needs and continuity states. Period wardrobe creates sourcing/fitting tasks; night exteriors use local daylight/weather and availability. Interior alternatives are suggested when appropriate. Score options present an original synth score versus simulated licensing needs for named source tracks.

Compare the same project using digital crowd augmentation: fewer physical extras for those shots, tracking/plate requirements and more post tasks. Compare a 2026 setting: period sourcing changes while zombie makeup remains. Compare stop-motion: physical actors/extras are replaced with puppets/animation tasks where applicable, voice casting remains, and live-action makeup chairs do not survive into the puppet pipeline.

Changing one choice must change its expected consumers, not every score. None of these presets guarantees an award or commercial success.
