# SLATE awards and festivals: approved design expansion

Saved November 13, 2026. This addendum preserves the owner's approved conversation design. It extends v7 and the Complete Lifecycle Standard. It is a gameplay specification, not a claim of new institution-by-institution research or implementation. All real category rosters, rules, schedules, fees and judging procedures need edition-specific evidence. Previously researched records remain usable subject to their documented coverage.

## Precedence and world history

This supersedes fixed future winners and separate recorded-versus-simulated results cards in the main game. Use one save-specific history. Events completed before the chosen start date are established history. After the start date, real releases, eligible projects, people and researched schedules establish the world; player projects compete and may replace nominees or winners within actual category limits. Do not guarantee the real winner or a player victory. Do not reveal future nominees from imported historical data before their announcement. Imported real nominees and winners are reference data, not mandatory future outcomes.

Keep provenance separate from gameplay records. Credits/About explains the fictional world; required source attribution still applies. Main ceremony screens show the save's outcomes naturally, not two competing histories. Preserve completed events during migrations. For old saves with conflicting results, prepare a preview and explicit reconciliation choice; do not silently erase wins, credits, payouts or player history.

## Institution and edition model

Each included institution needs its complete scoped edition roster, official display names, stable IDs and category changes over time. Distinguish film, television, animation, documentary, shorts and craft awards. Track recipient rules, project/person eligibility, episode or season scope, runtime and release conditions, territories, submission materials, fees when applicable, deadlines, voting/judging stages, nomination limits, ties and disqualifications where supported. Selection is not a prize; nomination is not a win. Never award every collaborator a prize that belongs to particular credited recipients.

Store announcement, opening, submission, qualification, shortlist, nomination, screening, ceremony and publication dates separately. Preserve organizer timezone and source precision. A date-only source does not establish a minute-level announcement. Mark an explicit simulation scheduling convention internally if a precise game trigger is required. Do not present invented dates as verified. Future editions beyond evidence use documented simulation scheduling rules, not copies masquerading as official announcements.

## Full player lifecycle

1. Discover relevant institutions and categories from the project and intended release, with an eligibility forecast.
2. Review each condition: satisfied, failed or unknown; show the actual reason and actions that could resolve it.
3. Plan qualifying releases, screenings and materials. Preview effects on other festivals, premiere status, distribution windows and deadlines before committing.
4. Identify submitter and credited recipients. Prepare screeners, credits and supporting materials as required by that institution.
5. Submit and pay applicable fees through the shared ledger. Record submission identity, time, documents and version of rules used. Handle incomplete, rejected, withdrawn and corrected entries.
6. Plan permissible campaign activity. Choose relevant screenings, publicity, appearances and priorities. Hire appropriate service providers only after their services and costs are researched or clearly modeled as simulated quotes.
7. Execute the applicable selection or judging stages. Some institutions have shortlists or several ballots; others do not. Avoid a universal procedure.
8. Announce the save's nominees at the proper event. Update calendars, profiles and notifications once. Allow meaningful next decisions without exposing winners.
9. Prepare attendance or representation, subject to scheduling, invitations and commitments. Attendance is not a universal condition of eligibility or winning.
10. Reveal results at the ceremony and assign them only to qualifying recipients. Persist the outcome and random state; reloading must not reroll it.
11. Apply category- and institution-appropriate effects on reputation, publicity, interest, offers and negotiation. Do not grant automatic profit or universal role boosts.
12. Settle campaign expenses and obligations; archive the save's nominations, wins and credits. Use the resulting career and project state in the next cycle.

Every stage needs player actions, prerequisites, responsible people, costs and payment timing, deadlines, persistent state, exceptions, recovery paths and downstream consumers. A screen label or numerical bonus is not completion.

## Judging and competition engine

Use relevant project qualities and credited contribution for each category; an acting award cannot use the same inputs as sound or screenplay without distinction. Define evidence inputs, category weights, institution-specific selection structure, bounded uncertainty and tie resolution. These formulas are original game design, not discovered formulas used by actual juries. Candidate eligibility is checked before competitive scoring. Candidate pools include real and simulated works supported by the world data, with coverage limits tracked internally.

Campaign activity may affect awareness and consideration within appropriate bounds, not guarantee merit or purchase wins. Budget constraints, availability and diminishing returns prevent endless expenditure from dominating. Do not back-propagate the same award into its own judging score. Seed and freeze stage outcomes at defined events so subsequent viewing and loading are consistent. Respect withdrawals and amendments through explicit transitions rather than silently replacing history.

## Festival lifecycle

Research and implement submission, section fit, programming selection, lineup announcement, premiere status, acceptance, screening arrangements, screenings, audience response, jury stages, prizes and aftermath separately. Distinguish competitive, noncompetitive and industry programs. A film can be selected and receive no prize. Buyer interest and distribution negotiations are possible consequences, not guaranteed offers. A public release or prior screening can alter eligibility elsewhere; query one authoritative release/screening history. Screening changes must update calendars, travel and contractual obligations.

## Freshness through causality

Each edition has a changing eligible field: newcomers, established contenders, varied formats, studios, project strengths and campaign capacity. A strong project may lose in an unusually strong field. Do not force surprise winners or prohibit repeat winners for novelty.

Season momentum can affect attention, campaigns and opportunities where applicable, but cannot mechanically guarantee later wins. Collaborators have independent bookings and projects, including conflicting campaign commitments. Near misses retain their underlying audience response and quality; losing does not erase a career. Recognition has distinct effects for a small festival, major award, nominated performer, composer or producer. Effects depend on institution, category, recipient, territory, audience and existing standing.

Track first nominations, first wins, repeated nominations, career breakthroughs and records from actual save events. Do not fabricate records without a known historical denominator. Annual ceremonies and familiar procedures may repeat realistically; freshness comes from changing circumstances and consequential choices. Vary natural wording from state without replacing gameplay depth with cosmetic text variation.

## UI and world integration

An institution page leads to editions and categories. Category pages show requirements, applicable dates, eligible work, submission state and announced nominees/results. Projects show their own campaign and entry history; people show their own credited recognition. Before announcement, show submission or stage status rather than future outcomes. Explain why an action is unavailable. Distinguish an unknown requirement from a failed one.

Keep identical essential information/actions on desktop and phone with readable layouts, no clipping, inaccessible controls or overlapping panels. Use natural labels such as Prepare Submission, Review Eligibility, View Nominees and View Results when their actions are available. Notifications must describe the actual event. Advance and sleep process milestones chronologically, with defined interruption policy and no duplicate outcomes.

## Verification and completion

Trace requirements through evidence → data → eligibility rule → UI action → event transition → saved result → downstream effects → automated journey.

Required journeys include: January start without spoilers; nominee announcement before result; player's project displaces a contender within category capacity; a real competitor defeats the player; repeat winner allowed; category-specific recipient credit; release invalidates premiere eligibility; fee posted once; withdrawn/corrected entry; schedule conflict with campaign appearance; campaign spending without guaranteed win; missed ceremony; advance across multiple events; save/reload determinism; year rollover and changed categories; post-award reputation/offer consequences; mobile parity; old-save migration preserving settled events.

For each institution record researched, modeled, implemented and tested coverage separately. Completion cannot be inferred from a passing build, a winner-only data row or a generic engine. Future sourced expansion must include all required categories and recipient rosters within its declared scope.
