# Named counterparties and complete deal lifecycles

## Evidence versus offer design
The real examples in data/research.json establish different scopes and scales. Sony/Appian Way is a feature first-look precedent; Amazon's 2017 agreements include combined film/TV and indie-film-only scope; Apple/Imagine is a scripted-film first look; Netflix/Ryan Murphy is an exclusive overall agreement. The reported upper value of $300m over five years is not a published first-look rate or guaranteed personal salary. WarnerMedia/Bad Robot covered several media and its official announcement did not disclose financial terms. Historical examples do not prove these deals are currently active.

A streaming service brand may not be the legal contracting entity. Store company ID, buyer label, business unit, medium, territory, authority to contract and dated corporate relationships separately. Start from the existing company registry; source current deal activity before claiming any company is currently offering that agreement. Naming a real company in the game creates a fictional offer, not a real endorsement.

## Distinct contracts
First look: first consideration of covered projects. Overall: broader services/output/exclusivity, according to terms. Development: specific development obligations and deliverables. Option/acquisition: source/script rights for defined term and scope. Production financing: project funding and approvals. Distribution/license: rights, territories, media, windows, guarantees and receipts. One should not silently substitute for another.

## Offer generation
Check real work record, reliability, role, company capacity, demonstrated audience, commercial results, relevant recognition, rights controlled, slate strength, collaborators, existing exclusivity and competing interest. Wealth affects ability to self-finance and bargaining alternatives, not an automatic talent score. Do not infer a studio's cash budget solely from TMDB release count. Use a versioned simulation capacity profile when actual internal budgets are private.

Companies evaluate genre/format fit, slate capacity, risk, cost and strategic need. A larger company can decline; a smaller firm can offer better creative terms. Explain the leading reasons to the player. A novice may get no first-look offer, a project development opportunity, or limited scope after a breakthrough. Exceptional contracts require exceptional evidence; campaigns must not let users farm trivial credits for repeated increases.

## Concrete initial calibration (original game design, not real price lists)
Use data/deal-model.json as a tunable starting model. It specifies annual overhead envelopes for first-look agreements across emerging/proven/established/elite profiles and separate overall-guarantee envelopes. These are unvalidated design numbers, bounded against selected historical context, not a researched market distribution. Run economy tests before approving balance. Player achievements select a band, fit/capacity/leverage select a position, and contract restrictions create tradeoffs. Seed any uncertainty once per offer, not on every open. No random reroll by declining and reloading.

Calculate offer components separately: guaranteed compensation; overhead allowance; discretionary/reimbursable development capacity; project fees; conditional bonuses; contingent participation. Do not sum a spending cap or speculative upside into guaranteed compensation. Display total guaranteed commitment over term, payment cadence and purpose next to potential amounts. Overhead may require invoices or restrictions if the fictional contract says so; do not automatically dump it into personal wealth. If a personal contracting creator has permitted overhead support, track a restricted business-use subaccount without pretending an entity was formed.

## Negotiation and agreement
Approach/representation → interest → nonbinding term sheet → review → counteroffer rounds → revised offer or refusal → explicit acceptance → active obligations. Record expiration, changes and accepted version. Negotiable fields: term, media, territory, exclusivity, existing-project exclusions, overhead, development approvals, consideration deadline, compensation, credits, approval rights, project-specific fee basis, matching/resubmission rights where agreed, repayment conditions, termination and renewal. Any matching provision is separate from a generic first look. A stalled negotiation can lapse; identical resubmissions cannot generate infinite leverage.

## Covered project cycle
Check scope and exclusion IDs before enforcing holds. Build submission package → acknowledge receipt/version → start consideration period → request clarification or decision → pass/develop/negotiate. Material amendments restart consideration only if agreed terms require it. A pass does not refund all development automatically; read contractual repayment and release conditions. An expired consideration deadline has the consequence defined by that agreement; never assume every agreement releases the project identically.

After a pass, record what rights and obligations survive. If turnaround applies, calculate documented recoverable development and trigger repayment at the specified event, not every time another studio opens a pitch. A new financier's money can repay old development once with a linked ledger transaction. Distinguish reimbursement from penalties and do not universally recoup overhead without a clause.

Acceptance of development is not greenlight. Move through budget, package, rights, cast/crew, approval and financing milestones. A TV studio can develop/produce while a separate network or streamer commissions/exhibits. Series renewal, ordered episodes and season obligations need their own agreement states. Film scope must not bind an unrelated television project.

## Performance, expiry and renewal
Track submissions, passes, development progress, greenlights, delivery, audience results, financial recovery, working relationship and breaches. A late milestone permits only the remedies actually agreed in the simulated contract. Expiry stops new covered submissions and recurring support according to terms; it does not delete existing project rights, debts or royalty obligations. Renew with an explicit new agreement/version. Different companies can seek an available filmmaker, subject to exclusivity and conflicts.

Use actual calendar-month schedules, anchored anniversary dates and idempotent period payments. Test Feb/leap-year handling, early termination, counteroffer expiry, conditional payments, restricted overhead, concurrent project cash needs and old-contract grandfathering.

## Downstream effects
Company overhead sustains real operating costs; development funds buy work that changes projects; studio notes create optional or contracted revisions; a greenlight schedules production; distribution rights constrain independent release; a pass frees only the allowed rights; successful projects affect future offers with bounded effects. Film awards affect relevant recognition without becoming a universal cash multiplier. Independently released work remains a viable route.
