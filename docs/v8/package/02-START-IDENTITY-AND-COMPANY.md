# Starting life and company lifecycle

## New-game setup
Identity → birthday/start date → citizenship and residence → language → starting city → inheritance review → confirm. Keep inclusive identity fields and all four roles. Validate ageOn(startDate,birthday)==19, including leap birthdays through the existing time engine. Do not force every birthday to equal the start date. Change start date with a preview if it makes age invalid.

Narrative: “Your family's wealth comes from oil. At nineteen, your inheritance is released. You have $25,000,000 available to begin your independent career.” Substitute chosen currency/amount correctly. This is a fictional scenario; it is not a universal inheritance law at nineteen. Treat estate administration and any taxes as settled offstage. No real oil family or company is attributed without the owner's choice.

The cash is owned by the player. Record source-of-funds metadata and one opening inheritance transaction. No recurring family income, no automatic share of future earnings, no second deposit for the same money when a company is formed. At a later start date while still nineteen, show it as an opening estate distribution already settled by start, not a fresh nineteenth birthday. Do not lock released funds behind an unimplemented trust system.

Bank account opening must respect researched local eligibility. For scenario initialization use a documented pre-existing eligible account where available; otherwise a clearly modeled fictional account, not an invented real bank approval. Large deposits are not automatically fully insured. Do not introduce random bank failure just to punish wealth.

## Independent creator, banner and entity
Default: personal independent creator. A production banner is an optional public credit/display name linked to that person. It does not create a separate owner, liability shield, incorporation, account or rights transfer. The player can create, cast, finance and release independent work without the game universally requiring incorporation.

Separate fields: display/banner name, contracting legal owner, residence, business jurisdiction, registration state, account owner, rights owner, project producer credit and distribution counterparty. A US sole proprietor example is supported by SBA; do not universalize it across countries. Unknown registration rules use an explicit simulation abstraction until researched, not an invented legal form.

Entity cycle: choose jurisdiction and purpose → review requirements and costs → reserve/display name if supported → prepare application → pay applicable fee → await approval → correct or retry → receive registration → open eligible business account → contribute capital → operate → report performance → grow/change → close or reorganize. Model rejection and pending state. Never spend an anticipated company balance before funding settles.

Owner capital moves personal cash into company cash and creates an owner-equity record, not company revenue. Owner loans are separate contracts with terms. Family investment directly into the company, if added later, would be a separate optional transaction with ownership/repayment terms; it is not the inheritance already credited. Default scenario uses inheritance then owner capital, resolving the user's two descriptions without double funding.

## Operations and growth
Company holds projects, employees/contracts, overhead, rights, counterparties and accounts. It can remain a one-person producer, hire a small development team or expand into a slate. Every hire has duties, availability, cost, payment dates and actual capacity effects. More employees do not instantly create quality or greenlights.

Project funds can be internal earmarks or legally separate production entities only where modeled; labels alone do not establish ring-fencing. Maintain personal/company/project ownership through casting fees, insurance, equipment purchases, rentals, rights licensing, music, marketing, distribution and merchandising.

Revenue cycle: customer/platform settlement → gross receipt and deductions → project recoupment waterfall → participants → company retained cash → permitted owner compensation/distribution. Never count a transfer as new revenue or a returned capital contribution as profit. Preserve unpaid expenses and contract restrictions before withdrawals.

Company closure: stop new commitments → settle/reassign existing obligations only with applicable agreement → collect receivables → dispose of assets → settle claims → distribute allowed remainder → archive records. Insolvency is distinct from low personal cash. Growth and closure must not delete credited works or outstanding royalties.

## Reputation and access
Inherited wealth grants purchasing capacity only. It does not grant craft, awards, personal fame, citizenship, visas, studio approval, guaranteed audiences or elite deal eligibility. Companies and the player maintain separate track records. A self-financed first film may be lavish and still perform badly; a modest project may establish a career.
