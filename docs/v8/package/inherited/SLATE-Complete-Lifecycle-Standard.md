# SLATE — Complete Lifecycle and Connected Depth Standard

Owner requirement saved November 13, 2026. Applies to every future SLATE design package and implementation review. This is a design requirement, not a claim that existing features are complete. Attach alongside v7; it clarifies and supersedes conflicting historical-outcome guidance without replacing its research.

## Governing principle

Every gameplay feature, subfeature and meaningful player choice must participate in a coherent, complete playable lifecycle. Nothing is decorative. Depth means meaningful alternatives, interacting constraints and lasting consequences—not just more labels, text fields or repeated options. The aspiration is continually expandable depth; no finite package should claim literally limitless coverage or perfect realism.

Explain every step from entry to exit, including preparation, decisions, execution, results, recovery and the next cycle. Every feature must connect to the same world, calendar, identities, contracts, financial ledger and saved history. Apply this to screenwriting, adaptation, casting, all production formats, equipment, music, distribution, merchandising, publicity, audiences, awards, festivals, careers, studio deals and their supporting systems.

## Required feature specification

For each feature and subfeature document:

1. Purpose and entry: what the player wants, where they begin, prerequisites, rights, access and resources.
2. Actors: who does the work; whom the player can hire, pay, negotiate with or delegate to in the fictional world; responsibilities and capacity.
3. Options: real alternatives with meaningful tradeoffs; suitable defaults, editable presets and format-specific choices; no ungrounded generic bonuses.
4. Sequence: states, milestones, dependencies, time requirements, parallel activities, approvals and deadlines. Identify optional steps and alternative routes.
5. Economics: who pays whom, what triggers payment, timing, deposits, fees, revenue shares, advances, recoupment, refunds and settlement. Distinguish cash from revenue and profit. Preserve the existing exclusion of tax simulation unless the owner changes it.
6. Engine: inputs, rules, constraints, calculations, uncertainty, persistent state, output and explanation of why that output occurred. Label simulated coefficients separately from researched facts.
7. Effects: immediate and delayed consequences for other systems, including money, schedules, inventories, rights, audiences, relationships, reputation and opportunities.
8. Exceptions: rejection, delays, insufficient funds, unavailable workers, capacity limits, quality problems, cancellation, disputes and recovery. Failure must lead to meaningful next choices.
9. Completion and continuation: delivery, results, settlement, residual obligations, archive, repeat use, expansion, renewal, retirement and the next cycle.
10. UI and language: exact entry points, screens, actions, feedback and natural wording. Preserve desktop information and action parity within readable mobile layouts, with no overlap or clipped essential controls.
11. Evidence: sources, edition/date, coverage, unknowns and research needed. Research real providers, current access routes and terms before claiming availability. Never invent private fees or imply a real commercial relationship exists.
12. Verification: an end-to-end journey, failure/recovery journeys, ledger checks, date checks, save/reload consistency and proof that each meaningful input changes the intended state or outcome.

A menu, named engine, mock result or screen alone is not feature completion. Maintain a coverage matrix: requirement → research → data → rule → UI action → state transition → downstream consumer → verification → status. Explicitly identify anything still unspecified, unimplemented or unverified.

## Merchandise: required example lifecycle

This is the required design scope for research and implementation, not a researched list of currently available vendors or prices. The actual route varies by product and agreement.

1. Choose the originating film, show, character or brand and confirm the game's merchandising rights, likeness permissions, territories, categories and approval obligations.
2. Identify audience demand, release timing, product categories and the intended commercial route: own products, licensed partner, made-to-order production or stocked inventory.
3. Develop designs, variants, materials, sizes, packaging and accessible product descriptions; hire designers where appropriate; price their work and revisions.
4. Seek suppliers or licensees. Compare quotes, capacity, minimum orders where applicable, samples, lead times, quality, territory and contract terms. The eventual package must name researched providers, what each actually offers, how access works and who pays each fee.
5. Obtain and inspect prototypes or samples, revise designs, secure approvals and commit to a production plan. A failed sample must affect schedule and cost.
6. Build the budget and price structure, fund deposits, set quantities or made-to-order capacity, forecast demand and define cancellation exposure. Forecasts remain uncertain.
7. Manufacture or activate on-demand production; track progress, defects, shortages, replacements and quality checks.
8. Choose storage and fulfillment arrangements. Separate manufacturer, wholesaler/distributor, retailer/marketplace, fulfillment operator and carrier; a single provider may perform several roles, but the model must identify which.
9. Secure actual sales destinations: owned storefront, eligible marketplace, retailer/wholesale placement, screenings/events or licensed partner channels as applicable. Define listing approval, assets, stock allocation, territory, exclusivity, fees and payout terms. Publishing a listing is not guaranteed demand or retailer acceptance.
10. Schedule the launch with the film/show campaign, prepare listings and marketing, decide preorder rules where supported, and allocate stock or production capacity by channel.
11. Process orders, payments and reservations; produce if made to order, pick/pack/dispatch, track delivery and support customer issues. Avoid selling the same unit twice.
12. Handle returns, damage, refunds, replacements, lost parcels and applicable product recalls. Costs, stock and customer response must change coherently.
13. Reconcile settlements: gross sales, discounts, channel deductions, production and fulfillment costs, royalties, partner splits, reserves and net cash received. Post each obligation and payment once.
14. Review demand and contribution margin by product and channel. Respond through restocks, new variants, price changes, revised promotion, alternative partners or discontinuation.
15. Close the cycle: remaining inventory, clearance or disposal, outstanding refunds and royalties, rights expiration, partner renewal, archived sales history and lessons affecting subsequent launches.

Connect merchandise to release delays, regional audiences, cast visibility, franchise recognition, marketing, cash flow and reputation. A hit film may increase demand; poor fulfillment may damage trust; excess stock ties up funds. These are mechanisms to specify and calibrate, not automatic guaranteed outcomes. Distinct products and channels require distinct costs, constraints and delivery paths.

## Living alternate history — owner correction

Real-world history supplies the starting world. Completed events before the selected start date remain history. After that date the player's eligible works can compete with real works, replace nominees or winners within category limits, and change downstream outcomes. Do not lock future awards to actual winners.

Keep announcement dates, submission windows, selection dates, screening dates and ceremony dates separate. Reveal nominations and results only at their appropriate events. Persist decisions and random state so loading or revisiting a screen does not reroll outcomes. Preserve source history separately from the save's simulated history. Reevaluate future dependent events coherently; never rewrite completed events silently.

Use Credits/About to explain the fictional simulation and real-world references. Keep routine game text immersive while meeting any provider-specific attribution requirements. Real people or businesses appearing in the simulation do not establish actual endorsements, offers or agreements.

## Research and execution ownership

Research and detailed design are prepared here for Claude to implement. Preserve existing verified research; return precise unanswered factual questions rather than generic blocked labels. Use exactly one coding agent, no subagents. Actual in-world teams may work concurrently; that does not authorize parallel coding agents.

Future handoffs must include concrete data, state rules, dependencies, screen behavior, natural copy, migration needs and acceptance criteria. Expand recursively wherever a decision introduces another meaningful process; reuse shared systems instead of creating disconnected duplicate engines. Stage delivery in dependency order while keeping the complete scope visible. Do not turn this standard into permission to erase working features or change saved player choices.

## Attach-to-Claude instruction

Treat this file as the standing SLATE completeness standard. Audit each feature against the required specification and lifecycle matrix. Preserve working code and saves. Implement connected, testable lifecycles in dependency order. Treat every omission as a named scope item, not as completed depth. Apply the alternate-history correction to conflicting fixed-result guidance. Report actual completed, tested and remaining coverage; never equate a successful build with complete gameplay.
