# Papers Research: What's Still Open

Researched November 17, 2026. A route whose fee a source gives but whose decision time none does is in the game as a
fee-only record (`src/data/papers/terms.json`, no `days`): the fee is researched and the time is the game's estimate,
said as one. Those are counted under `permitTimes` and `visaTimes` in `docs/v10/papers-estimates.json`.

Not recorded yet, as the sources conflict:
- Kyrgyzstan's e-visa: $30 to $82. Mongolia's e-visa: about $50, varying by nationality.
- Pakistan's e-visa: by nationality, $20 to $75, since the free visa prior to arrival ended on January 1, 2026.
- China's visas: by nationality and post. The Philippines' 9(a): by post (€30, CAD 43.50, AUD 54, $20).
- Malaysia's PLIK: PUSPAL RM 90 for filming, and RM 1,296 to Immigration by one consultancy.
- Cayman Islands' temporary work permit: a tiered CI$150 to CI$500 application fee from May 1, 2026; the entertainer's
  grant fee isn't published.
- Jamaica's work permit: JMD 17,250 to apply in 2019, nothing newer found.
- Chile's work authorization for transitory stays: 150% of the temporary residence fee for the nationality.
- Brazil's VITEM: US$100 at the Jakarta consulate for most categories; fees are each post's.

Found later the same day, also conflicting or incomplete (batch 3's research):
- The United Arab Emirates' mission work permit: Dubai's per-person figures (AED 200 a day talent permits, AED 3,000
  to 7,000 visas) come from consultancies and disagree.
- Switzerland's artists' permit: fees are each canton's (CHF 60 to 600 in guides) and times run from 2 to 12 weeks.
- Colombia's POA: filed 5 or 10 business days ahead by the film commission's two guides and valid 90 days; no fee
  found. Peru's SOL-ART: TUPA item 52, whose fee and deadline weren't reachable.
- Israel's B/1 for artists: NIS 1,300 is the 2023 fee for an expert's permit; nothing for artists; two weeks to
  three months.
- Morocco's shooting authorization: no fee published under law no. 18.23.
- Turks and Caicos: fees by occupation band, nothing for entertainers.
- Nigeria's e-visa: $160 plus $20 for US citizens by one guide, $253 plus $100 by another; stays of 30 or 90 days.
- Sri Lanka's ETA: free for 40 countries from May 25, 2026 and $50 for the rest, by one blog; not confirmed.
- Mozambique: the e-visa about $190 in most guides, $50 in one; the ETA $48 or MZN 650.
- Djibouti: a $23 e-visa, but stays of 31 or 90 days. Iraq: the federal e-visa $158 to $160 with insurance, $50 to
  $80 in others. DR Congo: $50, $80 or $100. Guinea: $80, raised to $100 for EU nationals from August 1, 2026.
  Burkina Faso: $50 to $143. Cape Verde: the EASE airport fee is 3,400 or 4,400 escudos.
- Venezuela: an e-visa for US nationals from April 6, 2026, with no published fee or time. Suriname: the e-visa and
  tourist card may have been replaced by an entry fee. Bolivia: US citizens visa-free from December 1, 2025.
- Nauru (AUD 50 to 100, or free) and Kiribati (no fee found).

Batch 4 (November 17, 2026, later), resolved where two sources now agree and recorded with both:
- Saudi Arabia: SAR 395 with the compulsory insurance on Visit Saudi's visa finder, SAR 535 being the 2023 price.
- Ethiopia: $62 for the 30-day e-visa since the August 2024 cut, three working days.
- Egypt: $30 for the single-entry e-visa from April 27, 2026. Côte d'Ivoire: €73 on SNEDAI, the accredited portal.
- Burundi: a $90 visa on arrival, 30 days. Liberia: USD 102.50 for the online visa on arrival, as its portal quotes it.
- New Zealand: from NZD 441 for the visitor visa, 80% decided within two weeks (Immigration New Zealand's own page).
- Ghana: $260 for the single-entry e-visa launched May 25, 2026, three to five business days. Indonesia: IDR 500,000
  for the visa on arrival, 30 days. Morocco: MAD 770 for the e-visa, about three business days. Ukraine: $20 on the
  Ministry's own page (its time is three or nine working days by different sources, so it's left the game's estimate).

Still open after batch 4:
- Iran: the e-visa is EUR 40 to 80 by nationality in guides, none official; one to four weeks, longer since January 2026.
- Malawi: the 2023 waiver was replaced on January 3, 2026 by reciprocal fees; $50 in the portal's FAQ, $75 on its fee page.
- Turkey: the e-visa is $15 to $100 in guides, and some say US and UK passports need none.
- South Africa: ZAR 190 or 425; five days to six weeks. Hong Kong: HK$230 before the September 2025 revision, and the
  Immigration Department's page gives no fee. (Both settled in batch 5, below: the 2026 gazette's R425, and the
  Immigration Department's fee table, HK$330.)
- Colombia: the visitor (V) visa's study and issuance fees are given as $52 to $56 and $52 to $177 by different guides.
- Argentina: the AVE is 20 business days on the consulates' pages, but its fee is USD 400, €368 or $150 by source.
  (Batch 5 recorded the consular tourist visa instead, whose fee the foreign ministry states: US$150. The AVE, for
  holders of a US visa, stays unrecorded.)
- Serbia: each embassy prices the type C visa itself (92 EUR in Baku, 96 USD in Washington, 88 CHF in Zurich).

Batch 5 (November 20, 2026), from the governments' own pages:
- Hong Kong: the ordinary visa, HK$330 since September 8, 2025, normally four weeks (Immigration Department).
- Macao: US$30 plus a US$20 relay fee through a Chinese mission, usually seven working days, 14 days of stay (Public
  Security Police Force).
- Taiwan: a single-entry visitor visa US$50 abroad (US$160 for US passports by reciprocity); no decision time
  published, so it's a fee-only record (Bureau of Consular Affairs).
- South Africa: the visitor's visa R425 in the fee schedule gazetted August 17, 2026; no decision time published, so
  it's a fee-only record (Department of Home Affairs).
- Argentina: the tourist visa's consular fee US$150, stays of up to 90 days; no decision time published, so it's a
  fee-only record (Cancillería).
- Not recorded, the governments' own pages refusing the tools or disagreeing: Cyprus (gov.cy and mfa.gov.cy refuse
  the tools; €90 for a Category C visa by search); Trinidad and Tobago (TT$200 single or TT$400 multiple by the
  Brasília FAQ, while the Ministry's September 2025 instructions say single-entry visas are no longer offered; 10 or
  14 business days by different missions).

Batch 6 (November 20, 2026, after the research recovery package), from the governments' own pages:
- Solomon Islands: the tourist visa US$50 plus the US$50 online lodgement fee, stays of up to three months, decided
  within 14 working days (Immigration Division's visitor visa page and its July 2026 short-term visa delivery policy).
  Since the Immigration Regulation 2025 there are no visas on arrival. The first route closed in full since the package.
- Uruguay: the tourist visa's consular fee US$54, not refunded on refusal (the Ministry's embassy page in Costa Rica;
  Mexico, Colombia and Guatemala give the same, Sydney A$84 and Havana €45 in their own currencies). No decision time
  published, only "apply at least 40 days ahead", so it's a fee-only record.
- Uganda: the Special Pass for work on a short contract, US$400 for three months (Directorate of Citizenship and
  Immigration Control). The 2004 regulations' US$50 is superseded. Fee-only.
- Rwanda: the class R Special Pass, for employment of up to 90 days, 50,000 Frw, valid three months (Directorate General
  of Immigration and Emigration). A search summary had read 250,000 Frw, which is class Q2's. Fee-only.
- Zimbabwe: the temporary employment permit, a US$500 statutory fee, not refunded (Department of Immigration). Fee-only.
- Mauritius: the Short-term Occupation Permit, a US$50 application fee (non-refundable, from December 1, 2025), and its
  US$300 permit fee paid only after approval in principle (Economic Development Board's guidelines, Annex 1). It's the
  first researched record with an issuance fee: `terms.json` records can now carry `issuance`, as route-scoped records
  already could, so a refusal costs only the US$50. Fee-only.
- Not recorded, from official pages that disagree or don't settle the route:
  - Trinidad and Tobago: US$143 for a multiple-entry visa at the New York consulate from January 22, 2026; one post's
    price, so it isn't used for every applicant.
  - Jamaica: PICA's US$100 port-of-entry visa, but multiple-entry visas cost JM$500 to JM$2,000 by nationality.
  - Suriname: the US$50 Entry Fee from May 15, 2024 (Ministry of Foreign Affairs) is for travellers who need the Entry
    Fee, not a visa.
  - Zambia: a temporary employment permit costs ZMW 18,000 to issue from August 19, 2024 (S.I. No. 25 of 2024), but the
    notice gives no application fee, so when it's paid can't be settled.
  - Bolivia: US$30 in the national tariff against US$50 at several consulates. Paraguay: US$65 at one consulate only.
  - The French overseas departments and collectivities: the 15-day decision time is the Schengen code's and doesn't
    carry over (an Assemblée nationale answer); a consulate says overseas visas can take longer.
  - Refusing the tools or not answering: Cyprus, Peru (gob.pe), Montenegro (gov.me), Mongolia's e-visa portal, Ecuador's
    Washington consulate, Tanzania's permit guidelines, and PacLII's copy of Nauru's regulations.
