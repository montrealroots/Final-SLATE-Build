## The command

Complete the current SLATE implementation and connect the remaining systems into a coherent, playable industry. This
extends v4; preserve its compatible requirements, existing saves and completed work.

Use exactly one agent. Inspect latest HEAD, open PRs, CI and repository instructions first. Reconcile work already
underway before editing. Do not restart the project, duplicate systems or overwrite unfinished changes.

The goal is complete, demonstrable functionality—not a claim of "100% perfection." Every mechanical choice must be
stored, read by its engine, produce an observable consequence and survive saving/reloading.

### 1. Complete source-specific adaptation

Replace broad genre templates wherever they substitute for understanding the selected work.

Each adaptation should reflect that work's actual premise, characters, relationships, setting, central conflict and
narrative scope where supported by available information. Clearly distinguish source facts from proposed adaptation
choices.

Populate the whole editable creative brief, including tone, pacing, structure, characters, locations and
film/season/episode progression. Preserve preview, field locks, selective changes and undo.

Do not treat dividing a plot summary into equal-sized chunks as meaningful scene or episode structure. Organize
proposed material around dramatic developments, dependencies, character changes, episode purposes and format
constraints.

Never invent source facts to fill gaps. Provide explicitly proposed choices when information is unavailable. Use
concise summaries and original adaptation planning rather than unauthorized reproduction of books or scripts.

Complete Guide Me for writing: commission/delegate development, drafts and revisions within an approved mandate, with
actual work, time, costs, review and delivery. The player can choose a work, approve the proposed brief, cast and manage
production without manually filling every field.

### 2. Tailor each production to its actual requirements

Build the production plan from:

- Format and length.
- Live action, 2D, 3D, stop motion or hybrid method.
- Script and scene requirements.
- Cast and performance methods.
- Locations, period, sets, effects and assets.
- Equipment, crew, budget and available time.
- Music, sound, localization and delivery requirements.
- Film versus episodic continuity.

A short, feature, documentary, limited series and ongoing series must not follow one generic task list.

Shared systems are appropriate, but tasks, dependencies, work quantities and resource bookings must reflect the
specific project. Hybrid scenes must combine the applicable methods without double charging shared work.

Preview consequences before approving a plan. Revisions reopen affected work while preserving completed costs and
unaffected assets.

Expensive equipment, famous performers and elaborate methods must not automatically improve quality.

### 3. Add real artists, recordings and composers

Build a sourced catalog of real recording artists, songs/recordings and film/TV composers. Include stable identities,
release dates, credited roles, versions, duration where available and provenance.

Connect people to the existing shared identity system rather than creating duplicate profiles for each role or studio.

Keep composer, songwriter, recording artist, performer, music producer, arranger, orchestrator, music editor and
supervisor distinct.

Real biography and credits are factual records. Availability, willingness, negotiation and fees generated for the game
are simulation. Do not invent a real artist's private pay rate or present a simulated quote as their actual price.

No real outreach or purchases are authorized. Do not include unlicensed audio or lyrics. Verify applicable provider
requirements before importing data.

### 4. Make music specific to the project

Replace simplistic genre-to-style recommendations with editable proposals informed by:

- Scene purpose and emotional progression.
- Character motifs and relationships.
- Rhythm, pacing and dialogue.
- Setting and intentional period choices.
- Existing songs and musical performances.
- Production resources and creative intent.

Do not assume every night scene needs tension music or every fantasy needs synth. Silence must remain a meaningful
option.

Support existing songs, commissioned songs, original scores and combinations.

Complete the loop: brief → shortlist → availability → request/proposal → negotiated terms → approved budget → sketches or
licensed selection → revisions/recording → cue placement → mix/stems → clearance and delivery → payment/credits → future
collaboration.

Guide Me may handle routine music work inside an approved mandate. Refusals, scope changes and overruns return to the
player.

### 5. Add first-look deals with real studio entities

Make researched real studios usable as counterparties in clearly fictional in-game first-look offers.

An offer should arise from the player's relevant work, delivery record, relationships, reputation and the studio's
strategy/capacity. Do not make every studio offer the same terms after a fame threshold.

Distinguish first-look agreements from options on individual works, distribution agreements, overall deals and
first-refusal rights.

Research these arrangements before modeling them. Supported terms should include:

- Parties and covered types of projects.
- Term and applicable territory/scope.
- Submission and response process.
- Consideration period.
- Any negotiated development support or obligations.
- What happens when the studio passes.
- Conditions governing taking the project elsewhere.
- Renewal, expiry, termination and surviving obligations.

A first-look agreement does not automatically finance, acquire or distribute every project. A studio pass should lead
to a coherent next route.

Track submitted projects, response deadlines, negotiations and obligations. Prevent contradictory deals and duplicate
commitments. Keep the player's approval necessary for accepting a binding agreement.

### 6. Let the player choose an independent release route

Add a clear Release Plan for self-produced films and shows, with meaningful supported routes such as:

- Festival-first.
- Direct or partner-assisted theatrical release.
- Distributor sales.
- Broadcast licensing.
- Streaming licensing.
- Transactional digital release.
- Supported self-publishing or direct-to-audience routes.

Research real platform access requirements. Do not imply that a player can simply upload a film to Netflix or another
selective buyer.

Let the player choose intended outlets, territories, dates, windows, versions, localization and promotional spending. A
buyer must accept a licensing proposal; self-publishing follows its own requirements.

Connect: release plan → delivery requirements → submissions/negotiations → acceptance or rejection → contract/booking →
release → audience and receipts → settlement → later windows → reputation and future opportunities.

Provide alternatives after rejection. Preserve rights already granted and prevent incompatible overlapping windows.
Support series as well as films.

### 7. Expand outside work and the living industry

Replace the tiny repeating pools of fictional production and company names with persistent productions and
counterparties connected to the world.

Outside acting, writing and directing jobs need project-specific roles, material, requirements, schedules,
negotiation, work, payment and credits. Do not gate every hired writing/directing opportunity behind releasing a
self-produced film; implement credible entry routes based on appropriate evidence.

The world should maintain active projects, releases, company slates, available talent, audience results, renewals,
cancellations, delays and future opportunities.

Events must respect dates, prerequisites, resources and prior outcomes. Do not reset the industry every year or force
random dramatic events merely to create novelty.

Renewal must create real obligations and next-season work. Cancellation must resolve existing commitments. Completed
player work should affect appropriate company interest and career opportunities.

### 8. Complete metrics and historical continuation

Keep recorded historical observations separate from simulated results.

Import actual box-office and audience figures only where available with their dates, territory, metric definitions and
sources. Missing data remains unknown. Do not call simulated figures Nielsen measurements.

Use a defined boundary per title/territory/metric between recorded history and simulation. Do not overlap or
double-count periods, expose future observations early, or rewrite settled career outcomes after a data refresh.

Connect simulated audiences and receipts to release access, capacity, scheduling, promotion, competition, reception
and contract terms. Preserve distinct broadcast and streaming metrics.

Record books must distinguish gross, admissions, rental income, profit and player earnings, and distinguish actual
from simulated records.

### 9. Finish money and equipment approvals

Complete company accounts and explicit transfers between personal, project and company funds.

Keep family gifts, loans, investments, project financing, equipment purchases, receipts and personal earnings
distinct.

Review equipment fallback behavior. Do not automatically rent a replacement package outside an approved spending
mandate. Show the shortage and offer a compatible rental, repair, reschedule or creative change, with its cost and
timing.

All payments, royalties, commissions, recoupment and refunds must post once and reconcile. No money should appear
because a project merely finished.

### 10. Restore and complete phone/desktop parity

The phone must mirror the desktop's visual identity, hierarchy, section order, information and available actions
within phone constraints.

Reflow layouts intelligently; do not shrink the desktop canvas, hide essential controls or replace it with a different
simplified design.

Audit every existing and newly added screen, including source creation, music, equipment, company deals, release plans,
financial reports and all dialogs/loading/error states.

Fix bleeding, overlap, clipping, alignment, spacing, inconsistent control shapes, unintended horizontal scrolling,
sticky elements covering content and image-driven layout shifts.

Verify phone picture installation through the real supported server/authentication path. Do not weaken access
protection simply to make pictures appear.

Test desktop Chromium, mobile Chromium and available iPhone Plus/Pro Max WebKit profiles, including landscape, long
text, touch targets, keyboard navigation, zoom and missing images. Inspect screenshots as well as measurements. Track
physical-phone testing honestly.

### 11. Prove the complete loops

Maintain an authoritative requirement/input/effect matrix. Reconcile stale ledgers and contradictory documentation.

Add complete UI-driven journeys for:

- Source selection through populated brief, delegated writing, casting, production and release.
- Real-composer consideration and simulated hiring through delivered score and credits.
- A song request refused, replaced and cleared for the chosen release.
- A first-look offer accepted, one project submitted, a pass received and an outside release pursued.
- An independent film and series choosing and completing different release routes.
- A paid outside job leading to a relevant future opportunity.
- Renewal/cancellation with actual downstream obligations.
- Equipment shortage requiring approval.
- Company-account transfers and settled player earnings.
- Phone picture installation and complete mobile project management.

Use fixtures only where explicitly disclosed. Do not claim a flow was played when its agreements or outcomes were
inserted into a save.

Run meaningful unit/integration comparisons, accounting invariants, save/reload tests, mutation checks for critical
rules, and full development/production Playwright suites.

Preserve performance budgets and investigate failures rather than deleting assertions or simply increasing timeouts.

### 12. Execution and delivery

Implement complete, tested milestones. Do not stop at an audit or plan, silently shrink scope, or call menus finished
features.

Commit and push verified progress. Merge only after required CI is green on the correct head. Preserve concurrent work
and existing saves.

At session limits, leave exact resume instructions and continue from them later. Final reporting must separate
implemented, verified, data-limited and unfinished work, with commit/CI links and concrete evidence.

The priority is a coherent game where selecting, making, releasing and building a career all connect.
